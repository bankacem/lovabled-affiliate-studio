<h2 id="care">Love Your Clothes: Care Instructions</h2>

<p>Once you have found that perfect printed shirt, you want it to last forever. The biggest enemy of prints is heat. Heat breaks down the binders in screen printing and fades the dyes in digital printing.</p>

<ul>
    <li><strong>Wash Cold:</strong> Always use the cold cycle. It saves energy and saves your print.</li>
    <li><strong>Inside Out:</strong> Turn the shirt inside out before washing to prevent friction against other clothes (which causes pilling and fading).</li>
    <li><strong>Skip the Dryer:</strong> I know it's convenient, but air drying is the secret to longevity. If you must use the dryer, use the lowest heat setting possible.</li>
</ul>

<div class="faq-section">
    <h3>Can I mix two different prints in one outfit?</h3>
    <p>Absolutely! The rule of thumb for mixing prints is to keep them in the same color family. Alternatively, mix a large-scale print (like big florals) with a small-scale print (like thin stripes or polka dots) to create visual balance. It’s a <a href="/blog/the-art-of-the-eye-roll-why-funny-dad-shirts-from-daughters-are-the-ultimate-power-move" class="auto-link internal-link" title="The Art of the Eye-Roll: Why Funny Dad Shirts from Daughters are the Ultimate Power Move">power move</a> that screams fashion confidence.</p>

    <h3>How do I stop my printed shirts from fading?</h3>
    <p>The best way to prevent fading is to wash your shirts inside out in cold water. Avoid harsh detergents with bleach additives, and try to line dry your garments away from direct scorching sunlight, which can bleach colors over time.</p>

    <h3>Are printed shirts appropriate for formal business settings?</h3>
    <p>Yes, but choose your print wisely. Stick to classic patterns like pinstripes, houndstooth, or subtle geometrics. Avoid loud, neon graphics or overly chaotic patterns in conservative office environments. Pair them with a solid blazer to tone it down.</p>
