  <section id="why-funny">
    <h2>Why Funny Works at the Gym</h2>
    <p>Gyms run on unspoken etiquette. Headphones on usually means "don't talk to me." A funny shirt acts as a soft invite — a low-stakes signal that you're approachable. Plenty of gym friendships have started over a shared laugh at a "World's Okayest Lifter" tee. In an environment where newcomers often feel watched and judged, a shirt that says "I don't take myself too seriously" lowers the social temperature for everyone around you.</p>

    <figure style="margin: 2rem 0;">

</figure>
<p>There's also a real psychological mechanism at play. Researchers Hajo Adam and Adam Galinsky coined the term "enclothed cognition" to describe how clothing can influence the wearer's mindset. The theory suggests that what we wear shapes how we think and feel about the task at hand. A shirt that jokes about the difficulty of the workout reframes the session — you're acknowledging the grind and laughing at it simultaneously, which can make showing up feel easier. Treat it as a useful nudge, not a performance enhancer: it won't add pounds to your bench, but it might make the walk through the gym door less daunting.</p>

    <p>Finally, funny gym shirts function as tribe markers. A niche joke about a specific lift or training style instantly identifies you to others who share that experience. It's shorthand for shared suffering — and shared suffering is the fastest friendship accelerator in fitness.</p>

  <section id="humor-styles">
    <h2>The Humor Styles: Find Your Lane</h2>
    <p>Not all funny gym shirts are created equal. Pick the lane that matches your personality — wearing someone else's humor style is the fastest way for the joke to fall flat.</p>

    <h3>The Reluctant Athlete</h3>
    <p>Shirts about the struggle: the snoozed alarm, the leg-day waddle, the eternal negotiation with the couch. This lane is universally relatable because the struggle is universal. It works best for beginners and anyone who trains because they should, not because they love 5 AM.</p>

    <h3>The Punny Powerlifter</h3>
    <p>Wordplay is the dad-joke division of gym humor: "I'm on a seafood diet — I see food and I lift it." Puns are safe everywhere and age well, but they need good typography to avoid looking like a 2012 Facebook meme.</p>

    <h3>The Self-Deprecating Specialist</h3>
    <p>"World's Okayest Lifter." "0% Bodybuilder, 100% Hungry." Lowering expectations is disarming — it invites conversation and makes you instantly likable. The risk is overdoing it; one self-deprecating shirt is charming, a whole wardrobe of them reads as a cry for help.</p>

    <h3>The Pop-Culture Gym Parody</h3>
    <p>Taking a famous logo, movie, or meme and "gym-ifying" it. This lane has the highest ceiling and the shortest shelf life — a perfectly timed meme reference is gold for six months and dated for the next six years. Buy these for fun, not as wardrobe investments.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="fabric">
    <h2>Fabric First: The Joke Needs a Good Shirt</h2>
    <p>Here's the thing: a funny shirt is useless if it's miserable to train in. The most common mistake is prioritizing the joke over the fabric. If you do high-intensity training, a heavy 100% cotton gag shirt becomes a soggy, abrasive mess within twenty minutes. Match the fabric to how you actually train:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Tri-blends (polyester/cotton/rayon):</strong> the sweet spot for most training — soft, stretchy, handles sweat far better than pure cotton, and gives prints a nice vintage look. Best for HIIT, CrossFit, and heavy cardio days.</li>
      <li><strong>Performance polyester:</strong> dries fast and never shrinks, but can hold odor and feel plasticky. Best for running and outdoor training.</li>
      <li><strong>Ringspun cotton:</strong> soft and breathable with a great hand feel for printing, but retains moisture. Fine for casual lifting and bodybuilding sessions; not ideal for sweat-heavy workouts.</li>
      <li><strong>Heavyweight cotton:</strong> durable and structured, but hot and heavy when wet. Better as a casual wear piece than a training shirt.</li>
    </ul>
    <p>Check the product page for the blank's fabric content before you buy — the same design printed on a tri-blend versus a heavyweight cotton is a completely different training experience.</p>
    <p>One more practical tip: check the blank, not just the design. Two shirts with the identical graphic can feel completely different depending on the blank the seller uses — a design printed on a soft tri-blend versus a stiff heavyweight cotton is a different garment in practice. Product pages usually list the blank brand and fabric content; if they don't, that's a red flag. And when the shirt arrives, give it the gym-bag test before its first workout: wear it for a full session, wash it once using the care rules below, and check the print. If it survives that cycle looking sharp, you've found a keeper worth reordering in other colors.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="print-care">
    <h2>Protecting the Print</h2>
    <p>You found the perfect shirt. It's funny, it fits, the fabric is right. Three washes later the graphic is cracking. Printed gym gear takes more abuse than your regular wardrobe, so treat it accordingly:</p>
    <ol>
      <li><strong>Turn it inside out:</strong> protects the print from the abrasive drum surface. This single habit matters more than anything else.</li>
      <li><strong>Cold water only:</strong> heat breaks down the adhesives and inks used in printing. Stick to 30°C (86°F) or lower.</li>
      <li><strong>Skip the dryer when you can:</strong> air dry if possible; otherwise use the lowest heat setting. Fabric and ink shrink at different rates, which causes cracking.</li>
      <li><strong>No fabric softeners:</strong> they can break down the bond between ink and fibers over time.</li>
    </ol>
  </section>
