  <section id="milestone">
    <h2>The "Dirty Thirty" Milestone</h2>
    <p>Thirty marks a psychological threshold: the twenties were for figuring things out, the thirties are for owning it. The "dirty thirty" label stuck because it's the rare milestone joke that's affectionate — it teases about aging while the whole party celebrates the person. That duality drives the design brief: humor about leaving the twenties, pride about entering the thirties, and a shirt good enough to wear in the photos everyone will keep.</p>
    <p>The shirt also functions as the event's visual anchor. Whether it's a weekend trip, a bar crawl, or a dinner party, a coordinated shirt turns attendees into participants and gives the photo album a through-line.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="themes">
    <h2>Themes That Define 30th Birthday Shirts</h2>
    <h3>Vintage birth-year</h3>
    <p>"Vintage — aged to perfection," "limited edition," "original parts" with the birth year in distressed retro type: the perennial bestseller. It flatters (you're a classic, not old) and stays wearable for years.</p>
    <h3>Dirty thirty humor</h3>
    <p>"Talk thirty to me," "level 30 unlocked," "RIP to my 20s," "death to my twenties" — the canon of thirty humor. Pick one lane: mournful-funny or triumphant-funny, not both on the same shirt.</p>
    <h3>Pop-culture nostalgia</h3>
    <p>Those turning 30 grew up in the 90s and 2000s, so era callbacks — sitcom riffs, retro gaming, cassette and VHS motifs — land as genuine nostalgia. Keep riffs original; printers won't run direct logo copies.</p>
    <h3>Minimalist and chic</h3>
    <p>A small "XXX" in roman numerals, a clean "thirty" in script, an "est." date on the chest — the quiet-luxury option for anyone who'd rather celebrate than advertise. These are the designs most likely to survive in regular rotation.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="customize">
    <h2>Customization: The Professional Approach</h2>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Palette:</strong> Two to three colors, high contrast. White on black is foolproof; gold on navy or cream on forest green reads premium in photos.</li>
      <li><strong>Typography:</strong> One display font for the headline, one simple font for details. Avoid novelty fonts unless irony is the entire point.</li>
      <li><strong>Placement:</strong> Standard 10-inch center chest, or a left-chest mark with a large back graphic for a modern feel.</li>
      <li><strong>Artwork:</strong> 300 DPI at final print size. Blurry text is the clearest signal of an amateur job.</li>
      <li><strong>Proofread twice:</strong> Custom pieces are non-returnable; have someone else check spelling and dates.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <section id="fabric">
    <h2>Fabric and Fit</h2>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Ringspun cotton:</strong> The default for softness and print quality.</li>
      <li><strong>Tri-blends:</strong> Softer drape and vintage heather look; thinner, better for fitted silhouettes.</li>
      <li><strong>Heavyweight cotton</strong> (6 oz+): Boxy streetwear feel, currently popular for bold graphics.</li>
    </ul>
    <p>Side-seamed "retail fit" shirts follow the body better than tubular promo tees and photograph better for the same reason. Check brand-specific size charts; when gifting, sizing up is the safer error.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="group">
    <h2>Group Coordination for Parties and Trips</h2>
    <p>The lead-and-support model works best: the birthday person in a standout color (white, gold) with the crew in a contrasting one (black, navy), sharing one design. It guarantees the guest of honor pops in every group shot. For trips, consider a back-print "tour" list of the weekend's stops — it becomes a souvenir with a story.</p>
    <p>Order group shirts at least three weeks ahead; custom production plus shipping leaves no margin for reprints.   <section id="printing">
    <h2>Printing Methods: DTG vs. Screen Printing</h2>
    <p><strong>Direct-to-garment (DTG)</strong> suits single orders, photos, and multi-color art; the print feels soft, "in the fabric." <strong>Screen printing</strong> suits bulk orders of 12+ with simpler art; it's the durability standard and usually cheaper per shirt at volume. For metallic or specialty effects, heat-transfer vinyl works but needs quality stretch vinyl to avoid cracking. Match the method to the order size rather than defaulting to one.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="gifting">
    <h2>Gifting Checklist</h2>
    <ol>
      <li><strong>Read their wardrobe:</strong> Neutrals and minimal outfits → typographic or embroidered design. Bold dressers → big graphic.</li>
      <li><strong>Confirm the size:</strong> Check a shirt they own or ask someone who knows. When unsure, size up.</li>
      <li><strong>Mind the timeline:</strong> Custom takes 1–2 weeks plus shipping; order early enough to avoid rush fees.</li>
      <li><strong>Add one personal touch:</strong> A name on the sleeve, the date in small type, or birth coordinates turn a shirt into a gift.</li>
    </ol>

  <section id="party-themes">
    <h2>30th Birthday Party Themes That Elevate the Shirt</h2>
    <p>The shirt works hardest when the party gives it context. Strong pairings for thirty:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Funeral for the twenties:</strong> Black attire, "RIP my 20s" shirts, dramatic eulogies for youth. The joke lands because everyone knows it's theater.</li>
      <li><strong>Vintage birth-year:</strong> Guests dress from the birth year while the birthday shirt leans into the retro design. Nostalgia does the decorating.</li>
      <li><strong>Cocktail evening:</strong> The minimalist "thirty" tee under a blazer — proof a birthday shirt can be part of a genuinely sharp outfit.</li>
      <li><strong>Weekend trip:</strong> One design for the crew, destination and date on the back. The shirt becomes the trip's artifact.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <section id="printer">
    <h2>Choosing a Printer Without Regrets</h2>
    <p>Not all custom printers are equal. Before ordering, check:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Proof process:</strong> A reputable printer sends a digital proof for approval before printing. No proof, no order.</li>
      <li><strong>Garment options:</strong> You want named blanks (ringspun cotton options), not mystery "premium tees."</li>
      <li><strong>Reviews mentioning wash durability:</strong> Print quality reveals itself after ten washes, not on arrival day.</li>
      <li><strong>Clear timelines:</strong> Production time plus shipping time, stated up front. Vague timelines become missed birthdays.</li>
      <li><strong>Reprint policy:</strong> What happens if they misprint? Good printers reprint their errors free; know this before you need it.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <section id="after">
    <h2>After the Party: The Shirt's Second Life</h2>
    <p>The best 30th birthday shirts earn a second life. How to give yours one:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Design for it:</strong> The minimalist and vintage designs transition to regular wear; the "RIP my 20s" joke shirt becomes a gym shirt or sleep shirt. Both are wins over the trash.</li>
      <li><strong>Care basics:</strong> Cold wash inside out, hang dry. Embroidery needs almost no care at all — another point for thread over ink if longevity matters.</li>
      <li><strong>The group photo:</strong> Take it early in the event while everyone looks fresh. It's the image that gets printed, framed, and revisited — the shirt is half its charm.</li>
      <li><strong>Store the memory:</strong> If the shirt is a true keepsake, fold it with the card and photos from the night rather than leaving it in the active drawer to fade.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <section id="timeline">
    <h2>Ordering Timeline</h2>
    <p>Thirty is the milestone people plan trips around, so the shirt timeline matters more here than for smaller birthdays. Four weeks out: lock the design and collect sizes. Three weeks out: approve the proof (read it aloud, check the birth year twice) and place the order. Ten days out: shirts in hand — try one on for fit and print quality. Party week: one cold wash inside out, then pack. If you're inside the two-week window, buy off-the-shelf in the right size rather than rushing custom; a clean ready-made shirt beats a rushed one with an error.</p>
  </section>
