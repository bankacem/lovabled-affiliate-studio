  <section id="match-personality">
    <h2>Matching the Humor to the Personality</h2>
    <p>The same 42 sayings above land very differently depending on who's wearing them. Quick guide:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>The roast-lover</strong> (gives as good as they get): milestone roasts and self-deprecating picks. They'll wear "My Back Goes Out More Than I Do" with pride.</li>
      <li><strong>The pun person</strong> (dad-joke energy): the puns section is their whole wardrobe. Safe for every audience, including grandparents.</li>
      <li><strong>The sentimental one</strong> (cries at toasts): go gentle — "vintage year" designs or warm party slogans, not roasts. The joke should hug, not sting.</li>
      <li><strong>The shy one</strong> (dreads being the center of attention): pick the understatement lane — "This Is My Birthday Shirt" lets them participate without performing.</li>
      <li><strong>The party animal</strong> (host of every group chat): squad shirts and bold slogans. They're buying for the photo as much as the person.</li>
    </ul>
    <p>When in doubt, ask someone close to the birthday person which lane fits. A 30-second check beats an awkward unwrapping.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="age-guide">
    <h2>Age-Appropriateness: What Flies Where</h2>
    <p>The same joke lands differently depending on who's wearing it and who's watching.</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>21st–30th:</strong> the widest latitude. Party humor, drinking jokes, and self-deprecation all work — just keep it legal-adjacent and kind.</li>
      <li><strong>40th–50th:</strong> roast the milestone, not the body. "Vintage" and "classic" jokes land; jokes about looks, weight, or memory cross the line unless the person makes them first.</li>
      <li><strong>60th and beyond:</strong> lead with affection. "It took 60 years to look this good" celebrates; "over the hill" mocks — use the former unless the birthday person genuinely owns the latter.</li>
      <li><strong>Kids' parties:</strong> clean only, and make the <em>kid</em> the hero ("Birthday Legend," "Level 7 Unlocked") — never the butt of the joke.</li>
      <li><strong>Office parties:</strong> stick to the work-safe lane above. If you wouldn't say it in a meeting, don't print it on a shirt for one.</li>
    </ul>
    <p>For the full rundown on birthday shirt styles beyond humor, see our <a href="/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-birthday-shirts-trends-customization-and-styling-tips">ultimate birthday shirts guide</a>.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="what-not-to-print">
    <h2>What NOT to Print</h2>
    <p>Some jokes aren't worth the laugh. Skip any shirt that touches:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Health and illness</strong> — jokes about hospitals, medication, or decline aren't funny to someone living it.</li>
      <li><strong>Weight and appearance</strong> — "fat" jokes about the birthday person are bullying with a gift receipt.</li>
      <li><strong>Divorce, job loss, or money trouble</strong> — punching down at real struggles ruins the party.</li>
      <li><strong>Profanity at family events</strong> — grandparents and kids share that room.</li>
      <li><strong>Inside jokes the guest of honor won't get</strong> — if they have to ask what it means, the gift failed.</li>
    </ul>
    <p>Rule of thumb: if you'd hesitate to say it in a toast with a microphone, don't print it in 72-point type.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="design-tips">
    <h2>Design Tips: Making Text Shirts Readable</h2>
    <p>The funniest saying in the world flops if nobody can read it from across the room.</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Short beats long.</strong> Seven words or fewer is the sweet spot. If it needs two lines, make the punchline the bigger one.</li>
      <li><strong>Bold sans-serif fonts.</strong> Heavy, simple typefaces read at a distance; thin scripts and novelty fonts don't.</li>
      <li><strong>High contrast.</strong> Light text on dark shirts, dark text on light shirts. Avoid tone-on-tone "subtle" prints for joke shirts — subtlety defeats the purpose.</li>
      <li><strong>Center chest, generous size.</strong> The joke is the design — don't shrink it into a pocket print.</li>
      <li><strong>Test at arm's length.</strong> Hold the design at arm's length on screen. If you squint, simplify.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <section id="gifting">
    <h2>Gifting & Fit: Getting It Right</h2>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Size up when unsure.</strong> A slightly roomy funny tee reads as relaxed; a tight one reads as a mistake. Check the seller's size chart — "unisex" cuts run roomy.</li>
      <li><strong>Order at least 2 weeks out.</strong> Print-on-demand production plus shipping needs buffer; custom text adds a proofing step.</li>
      <li><strong>Match the humor to the recipient, not yourself.</strong> The test: would <em>they</em> pick this shirt off a rack? If not, pick again.</li>
      <li><strong>For groups, coordinate.</strong> Matching "Birthday Squad" shirts in one color with the guest of honor's in another photographs far better than random picks.</li>
      <li><strong>Keep the receipt (digitally).</strong> Print-on-demand sizing varies by blank — a no-hassle exchange policy matters more than saving a few dollars.</li>
    </ul>

  <section id="faq">
    <h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
    <h3>Are funny birthday shirts appropriate for office parties?</h3>
    <p>Yes, if you stay in the work-safe lane — humor about coffee, spreadsheets, or "leveling up" is fine. Avoid anything about drinking, politics, or appearance.</p>
    <h3>How far in advance should I order a custom funny birthday shirt?</h3>
    <p>Order at least 2 weeks before the party. Print-on-demand production plus shipping usually takes 5–10 business days, and custom text may add a proofing round.</p>
    <h3>What size should I buy when gifting a shirt to someone else?</h3>
    <p>Check the seller's size chart against a shirt they already own and like. When in doubt, size up — a relaxed fit is forgiving, a tight fit is not.</p>
    <h3>How do I keep the print from cracking?</h3>
    <p>Wash inside out in cold water, tumble dry low or hang dry, and never iron directly on the design.</p>
    <h3>What's the safest funny birthday shirt for someone I don't know well?</h3>
    <p>Puns and wordplay. They're warm, inoffensive, and don't presume anything about the person's age, body, or life — "Nacho Average Birthday" works for almost anyone.</p>
