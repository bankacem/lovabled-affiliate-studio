  <section id="groups">
    <h2>Personalized Gifts for Groups and Occasions</h2>
    <p>Group gifts live or die on logistics: one design, correct sizes for everyone, and an order placed early enough to survive proofing.</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Custom family photo shirts.</strong> One photo, one design, matching shirts for reunions, holidays, or birthdays. Collect everyone's sizes before ordering and ask about the print method — photo prints need DTG or sublimation, not basic screen print.</li>
      <li><strong>Engraved family recipe board.</strong> A handwritten recipe engraved on maple or walnut, quirks and all. The version that preserves the original handwriting — not a cleaned-up typeface — tends to get the strongest reaction.</li>
      <li><strong>Coordinates keepsakes.</strong> The latitude and longitude of a first home, wedding venue, or favorite trip, embroidered or engraved. Double-check the coordinates and choose materials that last.</li>
      <li><strong>Custom map gifts.</strong> A star map marking a meaningful date, or a map puzzle centered on a hometown. Proof the date, location name, and caption carefully before approving.</li>
    </ul>

  <section id="how-to-buy">
    <h2>How to Buy Personalized Gifts Without the Headaches</h2>
    <p>Most custom-gift problems are avoidable. Here's the checklist to run through before any personalized order:</p>
    <h3>Fabric and material</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Apparel:</strong> 100% cotton or cotton blends take prints well and feel soft; polyester blends suit sublimation (all-over prints). For something worn close to the skin, mid-weight cotton is the safe default.</li>
      <li><strong>Leather goods:</strong> Full-grain leather engraves cleanly and develops a patina; bonded leather looks fine at first but wears poorly.</li>
      <li><strong>Drinkware and boards:</strong> Confirm whether the item is dishwasher-safe and whether the personalization is laser-engraved (permanent) or printed (can fade).</li>
    </ul>
    <h3>Sizing</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Always check the seller's size chart — print-on-demand apparel sizing varies between brands and often runs smaller than retail.</li>
      <li>For group orders, collect sizes up front; reordering one wrong size separately can cost more than the original batch in shipping.</li>
      <li>Jewelry and rings: get a proper ring size first. Engraved pieces are almost always final sale.</li>
    </ul>
    <h3>Printing method</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>DTG (direct-to-garment):</strong> best for detailed, full-color designs and photos on cotton. Soft hand feel.</li>
      <li><strong>Screen printing:</strong> best for bold, simple designs in larger quantities — cost-effective per unit for group orders.</li>
      <li><strong>Embroidery:</strong> best for monograms, logos, and text on hats, polos, and bags. Durable and premium-looking; not suited to photo detail.</li>
      <li><strong>Sublimation:</strong> best for all-over prints on polyester. The design becomes part of the fabric and won't crack.</li>
      <li><strong>Laser engraving:</strong> best for wood, metal, and leather — permanent and precise.</li>
    </ul>
    <h3>Proofing, lead times, and returns</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Treat the digital proof as part of the product: inspect it at full size for spelling, dates, initials order, and image resolution. Don't approve fine details on a small phone screen.</li>
      <li>Work backward from the occasion — production, proof revisions, weekends, and shipping all add up. Simple in-stock monograms ship fastest; made-to-order art takes longest.</li>
      <li>Read the return policy before paying: personalized items are commonly final sale, though seller errors and damage are usually covered separately. Save your order confirmation and the approved proof.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <section id="faq">
    <h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
    <h3>How early should I order a personalized gift?</h3>
    <p>Use the seller's stated production and shipping times, then add a buffer for proof revisions and weekends. A simple monogram on an in-stock item can ship in days; a custom portrait or engraved heirloom can take weeks. For a fixed date like a birthday or wedding, order at least 3–4 weeks ahead for made-to-order pieces.</p>
    <h3>Can personalized gifts be returned?</h3>
    <p>Usually not for change-of-mind — most sellers mark custom items as final sale. Seller mistakes, damage in transit, and items that don't match the approved proof are typically handled separately, so keep your order records and contact the seller promptly if something arrives wrong.</p>
    <h3>What printing method is best for a custom photo shirt?</h3>
    <p>DTG (direct-to-garment) for cotton shirts with detailed photos, or sublimation for all-over prints on polyester. Basic screen printing is better for bold, simple graphics and larger group quantities, while embroidery suits monograms and text rather than photos.</p>
    <h3>How do I make sure the personalization looks right?</h3>
    <p>Proof every character: names, dates, initials order, punctuation, and image quality. Supply high-resolution photos, confirm the monogram format with the seller (first-last-middle is traditional; first-middle-last is common in modern designs), and inspect the digital proof at full size before approving.</p>
