  <section id="how-to-choose">
    <h2>How to Choose a Cat Lover Gift</h2>
    <p>Before buying, answer three questions:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Is the gift for the human, the cat, or both?</strong> Choose apparel, décor, or jewelry for the human; choose beds, toys, or feeding accessories for the cat. First check whether the recipient already has a well-stocked pet setup.</li>
      <li><strong>What does the cat actually enjoy?</strong> Match the gift to a climber, lounger, hunter, food-motivated cat, or senior cat. Check size, washability, noise level, and whether supervision is required.</li>
      <li><strong>How soon is it needed?</strong> Buy an in-stock item for a deadline; choose personalized art only when there is time for a proof and production. Check the personalization cutoff, delivery estimate, return policy, and gift wrap.</li>
    </ul>
    <p>A gift for someone who loves cats but does not currently own one should usually celebrate the interest without assuming they want pet supplies. For a new cat owner, practical basics are more useful than another novelty item. In a multi-cat home, favor washable, shareable options or gifts that give each cat a separate place to eat, rest, or play.</p>

    <h2>Top 15 Cat Lover Gifts for 2026</h2>
    <p>These first 15 ideas are the most useful mix of personalized, practical, and humorous picks. Confirm the current price, dimensions, and delivery date before ordering.</p>
    <ol>
      <li><strong>Custom pet portrait pillow.</strong> Upload a clear, front-facing photo and check the artist's proof policy. A strong choice for a cat parent who likes sentimental home décor.</li>
      <li><strong>Cat mom or cat dad gift set.</strong> Combine a tote, tumbler, makeup bag, mug, or socks rather than buying a random assortment. Pick one useful item and one fun item for a more intentional bundle.</li>
      <li><strong>Personalized shallow cat bowl.</strong> A name or illustrated portrait makes a functional gift feel personal. Confirm the bowl's diameter, material, dishwasher guidance, and whether it is stable on the floor.</li>
      <li><strong>Washable orthopedic-style cat bed.</strong> Look for a removable cover, a low step-in edge for older cats, and a size that lets the cat turn around comfortably.</li>
      <li><strong>Stainless-steel water fountain.</strong> This can suit a cat already interested in moving water. Check replacement-filter availability, pump noise, cleaning access, and electrical-cord placement.</li>
      <li><strong>Funny literary cat tote.</strong> Designs such as "Pride and Purrjudice" or "The Great Catsby" work well for a reader who prefers a useful accessory over pet equipment.</li>
      <li><strong>Cat DNA test.</strong> Treat this as a curiosity gift, not a diagnostic tool. Read the test's privacy policy and confirm that the recipient is comfortable mailing a sample.</li>
      <li><strong>Supervised outdoor play tent or catio accessory.</strong> Choose a secure, escape-resistant setup and never treat a soft tent as a substitute for supervision or a properly enclosed outdoor space.</li>
      <li><strong>Custom fancy pet portrait.</strong> A Renaissance, astronaut, or superhero treatment is a good fit for a playful recipient. Ask how many revisions are included and what file or print size is delivered.</li>
      <li><strong>Cat exercise wheel.</strong> This is a space-heavy splurge. Measure the room, check the wheel's running surface and stability, and make sure the cat has shown interest in active play before buying.</li>
      <li><strong>Pet nose-print necklace.</strong> Confirm whether the maker needs a photo, an impression, or only the cat's name. Allow extra time for proofing and production.</li>
      <li><strong>Cat mom bracelet.</strong> Choose adjustable sizing and a metal finish suited to the recipient's everyday jewelry rather than relying only on the phrase "cat mom."</li>
      <li><strong>Comfy cat-themed sweatshirt.</strong> Check the size chart, fabric weight, wash instructions, and whether the print is screen printed, embroidered, or heat transferred.</li>
      <li><strong>Litter subscription or delivery credit.</strong> Practical only if you know the recipient's preferred litter type and storage space. A gift card can avoid an unwanted formula or recurring charge.</li>
      <li><strong>Personalized "If My Cat Could Talk" book.</strong> Verify the spelling of the cat's name, the available coat patterns, and the personalization preview before ordering.</li>

  <section id="practical">
    <h2>16 Practical Gifts for Cats and Their Owners</h2>
    <ol start="32">
      <li><strong>Interactive wand toy.</strong> Choose a sturdy handle and detachable attachments; store it away after play if the cat chews strings or feathers.</li>
      <li><strong>Treat-dispensing puzzle.</strong> Start with an adjustable, beginner-friendly design and use part of the cat's usual treats rather than introducing a new diet.</li>
      <li><strong>Washable tunnel.</strong> Look for a collapsible tunnel with a wide opening and a material that can be cleaned.</li>
      <li><strong>Cardboard scratcher.</strong> A replaceable insert lowers waste and makes the gift easier to maintain.</li>
      <li><strong>Vertical scratching post.</strong> Check height, base stability, and whether the recipient has room to place it near an existing scratching area.</li>
      <li><strong>Window perch.</strong> Confirm the window type, attachment method, weight rating, and cleaning instructions before gifting.</li>
      <li><strong>Felt cat cave.</strong> A quiet, enclosed bed can suit a cat that likes hiding; check the opening and interior dimensions.</li>
      <li><strong>Kicker toy.</strong> Select a long, stuffed toy for cats that grab and bunny-kick, and avoid loose embellishments.</li>
      <li><strong>Catnip or silver-vine toy.</strong> Interest varies by cat, so a small, replaceable toy is safer than a large multipack.</li>
      <li><strong>Grooming brush matched to coat length.</strong> Ask the owner what they already use and avoid presenting a brush as a solution for a medical skin problem.</li>
      <li><strong>Nail-trimming kit.</strong> Include a short how-to note and suggest asking a veterinary professional or groomer for a demonstration if the owner is unsure.</li>
      <li><strong>Soft-sided carrier.</strong> Only buy after checking the cat's measurements, the airline or vet requirements, and the owner's preferred loading style.</li>
      <li><strong>Carrier liner or washable mat.</strong> A lower-risk alternative when you do not know the carrier brand or size.</li>
      <li><strong>Food-storage container.</strong> Check the capacity, seal, and whether it fits the recipient's storage area.</li>
      <li><strong>Automatic feeder.</strong> Confirm the food type, portion controls, battery backup, and app requirements before choosing a model.</li>
      <li><strong>Portable water bowl.</strong> A useful option for travel, but choose a leak-resistant design and include a cleaning pouch if possible.</li>
    </ol>

      <li><strong>For a frequent traveler: a carrier organizer.</strong> Pockets for records, treats, and a collapsible bowl add utility without replacing the carrier.</li>
      <li><strong>For a cat sitter: a personalized instruction clipboard.</strong> Leave blank spaces for feeding, medication, emergency contacts, and the cat's hiding places.</li>

      <li><strong>For a cat dad who likes practical gifts: a durable cap or insulated tumbler.</strong> Select a design that fits his existing style.</li>
      <li><strong>For a cat mom who likes accessories: a subtle bracelet or pendant.</strong> Verify metal allergies, chain length, and personalization limits.</li>

      <li><strong>Under $20: cat coasters.</strong> Cork or silicone versions are easy to store and less fragile than thin ceramic.</li>
      <li><strong>Under $20: a cat treat jar.</strong> Label it clearly and avoid filling it with treats if you do not know the cat's diet.</li>
      <li><strong>Under $20: a small cat-themed notebook.</strong> A useful gift for tracking feeding, vet questions, or daily notes.</li>
      <li><strong>Under $20: a cat-themed bookmark set.</strong> A small, thoughtful pick for the reader on your list.</li>
      <li><strong>Under $20: a funny cat sticker pack.</strong> Low cost, easy to mail, and easy to love.</li>
      <li><strong>DIY: a photo album of the cat's milestones.</strong> Add dates, captions, and blank pages so the owner can keep adding memories.</li>
      <li><strong>DIY: a decorated treat jar.</strong> Use a food-safe container and make the label removable for cleaning.</li>
      <li><strong>DIY: a cardboard enrichment box.</strong> Use clean, plain cardboard with no staples, tape loops, or small parts left where a cat can chew them.</li>
      <li><strong>Spring or summer: a washable cooling mat or travel bowl.</strong> Check the care instructions and never leave a cat unattended with a new accessory.</li>
      <li><strong>Fall or winter: a washable fleece blanket or low-heat resting pad.</strong> Confirm that the product is made for pets and follow its operating instructions.</li>
      <li><strong>Valentine's: a cat-themed card with a shelter donation.</strong> A small donation in the recipient's cat's name pairs beautifully with a funny card.</li>
      <li><strong>Any season: a cat-themed gift card with a personal note.</strong> This is often the best choice when the owner has specific litter, food, toy, or décor preferences.</li>
      <li><strong>Any season: a framed "cat of the house" portrait print.</strong> A printable design from a local artist, framed yourself — personal without the wait.</li>

    <h3>DIY safety note</h3>
    <p>DIY enrichment can be inexpensive, but it should not be treated as automatically safe. Supervise cats with homemade enrichment and remove an item immediately if a cat tries to ingest it. Watch for small parts, strings, and ribbons, and rotate toys to keep play fresh. When in doubt, choose a commercially made toy, remove damaged items, and ask the cat's veterinary team about a health-related gift.</p>
