It’s not just about looks; it’s about your budget and the complexity of your design.</p><h3>Screen Printing</h3><p>This is the traditional method. Ink is pushed through a mesh screen onto the fabric. It’s incredibly durable and becomes very cost-effective for large orders (24+ shirts). However, you pay per color. If your design has 12 colors, screen printing will be expensive because of the setup fees for each screen.</p><h3>Direct-to-Garment (DTG)</h3><p>Think of this like a giant inkjet printer for clothes. It’s perfect for photo-realistic designs or small orders. If you only need five shirts with a complex, colorful image, DTG is your <a href="/blog/p-the-ultimate-guide-to-matching-best-friend-aesthetic-t-shirts-beyond-the-bff-cliche" class="auto-link internal-link" title="The Ultimate Guide to Matching Best Friend Aesthetic T-Shirts: Beyond the "BFF" Cliche">best friend</a>. The downside? It doesn't always have the "pop" of screen printing on dark fabrics.</p><h3>Embroidery</h3><p>For a Sweet 16, embroidery is usually reserved for the "VIPs." It adds a textured, high-end feel. It’s perfect for hats, hoodies, or that minimalist chest logo we talked about earlier. It’s the most durable option but also usually the most expensive per item.</p><p><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" class="text-primary underline underline-offset-4 hover:text-primary/80" href="/custom-apparel-comparison-guide">Learn more about the pros and cons of different printing methods here.</a></p><h2>The Logistics of Group Ordering</h2><p>Let’s be real: trying to get sizes from thirty different teenagers is like herding cats. Here is my "pro-tip" for managing the chaos:</p><ol><li><p><strong>The "Rule of Averages":</strong> If you don't have exact sizes, order a "Bell Curve" distribution. For a group of 20, that usually looks like: 2 Small, 6 Medium, 8 Large, 3 XL, 1 XXL. People usually prefer a shirt that’s too big over one that’s too small.</p></li><li><p><strong>Set a Hard Deadline:</strong> Tell your guests you need their size a full week before you actually need to place the order.</p></li><li><p><strong>Order Spares:</strong> Always order 2-3 extra shirts in Large. Someone will lose theirs, or a last-minute guest will be added to the list.</p></li></ol><p>What’s interesting is that many modern print shops now offer "online stores" for events. They’ll set up a temporary link where guests can go and select their own sizes and pay individually.
