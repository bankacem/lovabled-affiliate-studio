    <h2>The Five Spooky Shirt Archetypes</h2>
    <p>Every spooky tee on the market falls into one of five buckets. Identify your bucket before you shop — it narrows thousands of options down to a shortlist in seconds.</p>

    <h3>1. Gothic Elegance</h3>
    <p>Victorian-inspired linework, lace-like filigree, skulls entwined with roses, ravens and candelabras. The palette is disciplined: black, bone white, deep burgundy, antique gold. Gothic elegance reads as sophisticated rather than costume-y, which makes it the easiest spooky style to wear to dinner, galleries, or a smart-casual office. Look for fine-line printing — the detail is the whole point, so cheap blurry prints ruin this archetype faster than any other.</p>

    <h3>2. Retro Horror</h3>
    <p>Distressed prints pulled from the golden age of horror cinema: haunted houses, vintage monster portraits, grainy 80s slasher typography. The "washed" or acid-wash fabric treatment matters here — a crisp new print on a bright white tee reads as merch-table, while the faded treatment sells the nostalgia. This is the archetype for horror-movie nights, conventions, and anyone whose Halloween personality is "I was into this before it was cool."</p>

    <h3>3. Pastel Goth</h3>
    <p>Macabre imagery in soft colors — lavender ghosts, mint bats, blush-pink skeletons. Pastel goth keeps the spooky motifs but drops the menace, which makes it the most giftable and most office-friendly of the dark styles. It photographs beautifully, so it dominates social feeds every October. The key quality check: the pastel inks should be opaque, not washed out — thin pastel prints look faded after two washes.</p>

    <h3>4. Occult Minimal</h3>
    <p>Thin white or gold linework on black: moon phases, tarot motifs, alchemy symbols, palmistry diagrams. Occult minimal is the quiet-luxury version of spooky — it reads as intentional design rather than holiday novelty, which is why it gets worn year-round. Because the designs are simple, print quality is everything: lines should be razor-sharp, not pixelated. Zoom into product photos before buying.</p>

    <h3>5. Cryptid &amp; Folk Horror</h3>
    <p>Bigfoot, mothman, the Jersey devil — rendered in the style of vintage national-park souvenirs or woodcut illustrations. This archetype leans outdoorsy and conversational; it's a story printed on a shirt. It skews slightly more niche, which is actually an advantage: you're unlikely to see three other people wearing the same design at a party.</p>

  <section id="styling">
    <h2>How to Style Each Archetype</h2>
    <p>A spooky shirt is only as good as the outfit around it. One formula per archetype:</p>
    <h3>Gothic elegance → monochrome tailoring</h3>
    <p>Tuck it into black high-waisted trousers or a midi skirt, add a structured blazer and boots. The tailored pieces keep the skull-and-roses graphic reading as fashion rather than costume.</p>
    <h3>Retro horror → vintage casual</h3>
    <p>Oversized fit, distressed denim, canvas sneakers or combat boots. A flannel tied around the waist completes the 90s-thrift-store energy this archetype wants.</p>
    <h3>Pastel goth → soft contrast</h3>
    <p>Pair with light-wash jeans or a lavender skirt and white sneakers. Keep accessories delicate — the softness is the point, so heavy hardware fights it.</p>
    <h3>Occult minimal → dark basics</h3>
    <p>Black jeans, black boots, one silver ring. Let the linework be the only detail. This archetype also layers best: under an open overshirt or a long coat in cold weather.</p>
    <h3>Cryptid folk → outdoorsy layers</h3>
    <p>With cargo pants or corduroys, a canvas jacket, and hiking-style boots. The national-park-poster vibe extends naturally into the rest of the outfit.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="buying">
    <h2>The Buying Checklist</h2>
    <p>Run through this before you check out — it filters out 90% of disappointing purchases:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><p><strong>Fabric first:</strong> 100% ringspun cotton for softness, or a cotton-poly tri-blend for a draped vintage feel. Avoid thin, scratchy blanks no matter how good the graphic is.</p></li>
      <li><p><strong>Print method:</strong> screen printing lasts longest; direct-to-garment (DTG) handles complex multi-color art but fades faster; embroidery is the most durable for small minimalist designs.</p></li>
      <li><p><strong>Zoom the graphic:</strong> fine-line gothic and occult designs must be sharp in the product photos. Blurry preview, blurry shirt.</p></li>
      <li><p><strong>Check the size chart:</strong> print-on-demand blanks vary by brand. Measure a shirt you already own and compare — don't guess.</p></li>
      <li><p><strong>Color honesty:</strong> "vintage black" and "washed" blanks photograph lighter than they arrive. If you want true black, order true black.</p></li>
      <li><p><strong>Re-wear test:</strong> ask whether you'd wear it in March. If yes, it's a wardrobe piece; if no, it's a costume — price it accordingly.</p></li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <section id="year-round">
    <h2>Wearing Spooky Shirts Beyond October</h2>
    <p>Three archetypes earn year-round rotation without explanation: gothic elegance (it reads as alternative fashion, not Halloween), occult minimal (it reads as design), and cryptid folk (it reads as outdoorsy). Retro horror and pastel goth are more seasonal — perfectly fine in October, slightly costume-adjacent in April. If year-round wear matters to you, weight your choice toward the first three.   <section id="faq">
    <h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
    <h3>What's the difference between spooky shirts and gothic shirts?</h3>
    <p>Gothic is one archetype within spooky. "Spooky shirt" is the umbrella term covering gothic elegance, retro horror, pastel goth, occult minimal, and cryptid styles — gothic is the Victorian-inspired, black-and-bone-white branch of that family.</p>
    <h3>Can I wear a spooky shirt to work?</h3>
    <p>Occult minimal and gothic elegance work in most casual and business-casual offices, especially layered under a blazer. Retro horror and pastel goth are better saved for casual Fridays or creative workplaces.</p>
    <h3>How do I keep the print from cracking?</h3>
    <p>Wash inside out in cold water and air dry when possible. Never iron directly over the graphic — flip the shirt inside out first or use a steamer.</p>
    <h3>Are pastel goth shirts only for Halloween?</h3>
    <p>They peak in October, but the soft palettes (lavender, mint, blush) work year-round as alternative fashion. The motifs are spooky; the colors aren't seasonal.</p>
    <h3>What should I look for in a vintage-style horror print?</h3>
    <p>Deliberate distressing (not a blurry low-res file), a washed or garment-dyed blank, and licensing honesty — genuine vintage reprints credit the film or artist. When in doubt, independent artists on print-on-demand marketplaces usually show the sharpest previews.</p>
    <h3>Which spooky archetype suits a first-time buyer?</h3>
    <p>Occult minimal if you want year-round wear, pastel goth if you want October maximum impact with minimum risk. Both are forgiving on fit and easy to style.</p>
