  <section id="lore">
    <h2>Frog Meme Lore: Three Frogs, Three Moods</h2>
    <p>Frog memes didn't appear out of nowhere — the frog has been internet royalty for over a decade, from reaction images to cottagecore aesthetics. The shirt designs that last are the ones built on a recognizable "character" with a clear attitude. Three of the most enduring frog personas:</p>

    <figure style="margin: 2rem 0;">

<h3>1. The Kerfuffle Frog (Sarcastic Chaos)</h3>
    <p>"Off to Cause a Kerfuffle" — a cheeky frog with a mischievous attitude, announcing its intention to create small, harmless trouble. This is the patron saint of group chats, Monday mornings, and anyone whose personality is 10% menace, 90% lovable. The humor is ironic and self-aware: the "chaos" is always playful, never mean. It resonates with people who appreciate witty, personality-driven visual humor and want their shirt to do the talking.</p>

    <h3>2. The Praise-the-Lord Frog (Wholesome Faith Humor)</h3>
    <p>The same frog energy, redirected upward: a cheerful frog paired with joyful praise, blending gentle humor with genuine positivity. Faith-based meme designs have found a real audience because they make belief feel approachable rather than formal — a shirt that sparks a smile and maybe a conversation. It works as everyday casual wear and as a thoughtful gift for anyone who appreciates Christian humor expressed lightly.</p>

    <h3>3. The We-Ride-at-Dawn Frog (Retro Battle Cry)</h3>
    <p>The most dramatic of the three: a frog (often alongside a goose) rendered in bold retro style, declaring "We Ride at Dawn" like a tiny general leading a charge into... brunch, errands, or the group project. The comedy comes from the mismatch — epic medieval energy applied to completely ordinary plans. Paired with distressed vintage graphics, it's the design equivalent of a rallying cry for your most unserious commitments. The goose companion matters: geese are nature's chaos agents, so a frog-and-goose duo reads as a fully staffed mischief department.</p>

    <h3>Why Frogs, Though?</h3>
    <p>The frog works as a meme mascot for a few structural reasons. It's expressive without being human — big eyes and a wide mouth read emotion instantly. It's small and unthreatening, which makes "tough" poses funny (a frog declaring war is inherently absurd). And it's endlessly adaptable: the same frog silhouette can be sarcastic, wholesome, anxious, or heroic depending on the caption and art style. That's why frog designs have outlasted countless other meme animals — the character is a template, not a one-liner.</p>

  <section id="styles">
    <h2>Design Styles That Work on Shirts</h2>
    <p>Not every meme survives the jump from screen to fabric. The frog designs that sell share a few traits:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Bold, readable text:</strong> The phrase is half the joke. Blocky retro lettering or clean bold type reads from across the room; thin script fonts kill the punchline.</li>
      <li><strong>Expressive character art:</strong> The frog needs a face with attitude — mischievous, cheerful, or determined. A generic frog clipart with text slapped on it doesn't land.</li>
      <li><strong>Retro treatment:</strong> Distressed textures, vintage color palettes, and 70s-style typography give meme designs a "classic tee" feel that outlasts the meme's news cycle.</li>
      <li><strong>High contrast:</strong> Dark design on a light shirt (or vice versa). Meme shirts live or die on legibility — if people squint, the joke's over.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <section id="buying">
    <h2>Meme Shirt Buying Guide</h2>
    <p>A meme shirt has one job: deliver the joke clearly and survive the wash. Here's how to judge one before you buy:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Zoom the thumbnail:</strong> If the design isn't legible as a small product thumbnail, it won't be legible on a chest either. The best meme shirts pass the "phone screen test."</li>
      <li><strong>Check the blank:</strong> Ring-spun cotton or cotton-poly blends feel better and hold prints longer than stiff, cheap carded cotton. Garment-dyed blanks (like Comfort Colors-style) give that lived-in vintage feel from day one.</li>
      <li><strong>Match print to design:</strong> Bold, simple graphics do well with screen printing; detailed, colorful frog illustrations are better as direct-to-garment (DTG) prints.</li>
      <li><strong>Size honestly:</strong> Most meme tees are unisex (men's) sizing. Size down for a fitted look, or stay true to size for the relaxed streetwear drape that suits meme aesthetics.</li>
      <li><strong>Wash cold, inside-out:</strong> The universal rule. Meme shirts with distressed retro effects actually age well — a little fading suits the aesthetic.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
