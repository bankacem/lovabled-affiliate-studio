  <section id="why-humor">
    <h2>Why Humor Is a Survival Tool in Nursing</h2>
    <p>Ask any nurse about gallows humor and you'll get a knowing laugh. In high-acuity environments, humor does real psychological work: it defuses tension after hard moments, builds instant camaraderie between colleagues who've been through the same chaos, and humanizes clinicians in the eyes of nervous patients.</p>
    <p>A funny nurse shirt is a wearable version of that coping mechanism. A tee that reads "Night Shift: Working While You Sleep" or "Caffeine, Charting, and Chaos" is a silent handshake with every nurse who passes by — an instant signal that you're part of the same in-group. It's also why these shirts make such good gifts: you're not just giving clothing, you're acknowledging the grind.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="styles">
    <h2>Humor Styles: From Puns to Dark Sarcasm</h2>
    <p>The funny nurse shirt world has moved far beyond generic "Nurse Life" graphics. Today's designs are hyper-specialized:</p>
    <h3>Sarcastic Puns</h3>
    <p>The classics with an edge: "I Can't Fix Stupid, But I Can Sedate It," "Trust Me, I'm a Nurse." High humor value, but keep them out of patient-facing areas — breakroom and off-duty only.</p>
    <h3>Vintage and Retro Graphics</h3>
    <p>Muted 70s-style typography and distressed prints are the current aesthetic trend. They read more like streetwear than novelty tees, which makes them the most versatile style for off-duty wear.</p>
    <h3>Specialty and Unit Designs</h3>
    <p>ER "chaos coordinators," ICU crews, night-shift "zombies," L&D teams — unit-specific jokes build the strongest camaraderie. These make the best gifts because they show you know exactly what kind of nursing they do.</p>
    <h3>Minimalist Typography</h3>
    <p>Clean text designs ("Nurse," "RN," small heart graphics) that work as under-scrub base layers. The least "funny" of the bunch, but the most wearable at work.</p>
    <h3>Holiday and Seasonal</h3>
    <p>Halloween ghosts, Christmas scrubs humor, Nurses Week specials. High engagement with patients (especially peds), but limited to their season.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="fabric">
    <h2>Fabric and Fit for 12-Hour Shifts</h2>
    <p>A funny shirt is useless if it feels like sandpaper four hours into a shift. For nursing apparel, the specs matter as much as the joke:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Tri-blends (polyester/cotton/rayon):</strong> the best all-around choice — breathable, moisture-wicking, drapes well, and resists shrinkage in hot hospital laundry cycles.</li>
      <li><strong>100% ring-spun cotton:</strong> soft and durable, but holds sweat more than blends. Fine for off-duty wear.</li>
      <li><strong>Pre-shrunk fabrics:</strong> essential. Nobody wants their favorite tee turning into a crop top after one wash.</li>
      <li><strong>Fit:</strong> slim or retail fit if worn under scrubs (avoids bulk); oversized heavyweight cotton for lounging and days off.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <section id="dress-code">
    <h2>Navigating Workplace Dress Codes</h2>
    <p>Not every facility is fine with a shirt that mentions sedation jokes. Before wearing a graphic tee to work:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Read the handbook:</strong> most modern facilities allow tees as undershirts as long as sleeves don't interfere with hygiene protocols.</li>
      <li><strong>Know your unit:</strong> pediatric units tend to be the most lenient — funny, colorful designs can even help distract young patients. High-acuity units (ICU, oncology) call for restraint.</li>
      <li><strong>Keep dark humor off the floor:</strong> save the edgiest designs for breakrooms, post-shift hangouts, and days off.</li>
      <li><strong>When in doubt, go minimalist:</strong> a small "RN" or heart graphic under scrubs is never a problem.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <section id="care">
    <h2>Making Your Shirt Last: Care Guide</h2>
    <p>Hospital laundry is brutal, and home washers aren't much kinder to printed graphics. Protect your investment:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Always wash inside out in cold water.</strong> This single habit does more for print longevity than anything else — it keeps the graphic from rubbing against other garments.</li>
      <li><strong>Tumble dry low or air dry.</strong> High heat is the enemy of both cotton fibers and printed ink. If you air dry, hang the shirt by the hem rather than the shoulders to avoid stretching the neckline.</li>
      <li><strong>Never iron directly on the print.</strong> If you need to de-wrinkle, flip the shirt inside out and iron on the reverse side with low heat.</li>
      <li><strong>Skip the bleach and harsh stain removers</strong> on printed areas — they fade graphics fast. Treat stains before washing with a gentle pre-treater instead.</li>
      <li><strong>Rotate your favorites.</strong> A shirt worn every week wears out in months; one in a rotation of five lasts for years.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <section id="nurses-week">
    <h2>Nurses Week: The Prime Gifting Window</h2>
    <p>Nurses Week (May 6–12) is the single best occasion for a funny nurse shirt. Facilities run spirit days, units organize group orders, and the whole profession is in celebration mode — a funny tee lands harder here than on any random Tuesday. If you're ordering for a team, start 3–4 weeks early: print-on-demand production plus shipping needs lead time, and group orders always have one person who forgets to submit their size. Matching unit shirts for Nurses Week photos have become a tradition in many hospitals — the funnier and more unit-specific, the better the photo.</p>
    <p>Graduation season (April–July) is the second-biggest window: nursing school grads and newly minted RNs love shirts that acknowledge the grind they just survived. For group orders, collect sizes early and order one extra in the most common size — there's always someone who joins late, and a spare shirt turns a logistical headache into a non-issue.</p>
